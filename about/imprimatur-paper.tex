\begin{abstract}
A paper about a Lean development restates its definitions and theorems in
mathematical notation. imprimatur generates those restatements from the
development, keeps the text the authors wrote around them, and checks that
the paper still says what Lean proved. Between the development and the
paper there is one file, the bundle, and we give it a type and a meaning:
what it says of each theorem and definition, and when that is true of the
development. We then state what a successful \texttt{imprimatur check}
guarantees, in two stages: first for the mathematics the paper generates,
then for the prose its authors write and attest; and we prove it, for a
model of imprimatur's splicer in Lean, as one theorem: a paper that checks
clean against a true bundle is true. This paper is itself built with
imprimatur: every displayed definition and theorem below is generated from
that Lean development, and every hand-written claim about it is checked
against it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A paper and its Lean development drift apart. A theorem is weakened in Lean
and not in the paper; a definition gains a case; a lemma the paper cites is
proved with \texttt{sorry}. imprimatur closes the gap in two ways. Blocks of
the paper are \emph{generated}: the extractor renders a Lean constant's
statement in the paper's notation, and the splicer writes it into the paper,
keeping any text the authors wrote in its \emph{slots}. Hand-written prose
about a constant is \emph{attested}: a \verb|\leancheck| beside it records
the hash of the statement the prose was checked against, and the check
fails if the statement changes.

imprimatur is two programs. The extractor runs inside Lean, at the
development's own toolchain, and reads its elaborated environment. The
splicer edits \TeX{} and uses nothing of Lean but its standard library, so
one splicer serves every toolchain. All that passes between them is the
bundle, a JSON file. So there are three things, and two relations between
them:
\begin{center}
  the development $W$
  $\xrightarrow{\;\text{the bundle is true of it}\;}$
  the bundle $b$
  $\xrightarrow{\;\text{the paper checks clean}\;}$
  the paper $d$.
\end{center}
What does it mean for the paper to be correct? That what it shows is what
Lean proved. We say this without mentioning the bundle (what a paper claims
of a development, Section~\ref{sec:check}), say what makes a bundle true of
a development (Section~\ref{sec:meaning}), and prove that the two relations
compose: \emph{a paper that checks clean against a true bundle is true}. The
proof is for a model of the splicer, in which a document is already parsed
into pieces. It comes in two stages. The generated mathematics needs only
the renderings in the bundle. The attested prose needs the hashes, and we
say why. A second theorem, about the notation, says that a rendering can be
read back as the statement it came from; imprimatur checks it for each
formula it renders (Section~\ref{sec:faithful}).

\section{Documents}

A document is a list of pieces: hand-written text, generated blocks, and
checks on hand-written prose. A block shows a key, the name of a Lean
constant or the title of a group, in a view, such as a formula, a sentence
or a figure of inference rules. Its body is a list of segments: text
generated from Lean, and slots, whose text the authors own.

\begin{leangrammar}[sum=1zCWSr9i5v]{SpliceModel.Seg}{Segments}
\label{gram:SpliceModel.Seg}\begin{LeanGrammar}
\LeanProduction{\varsigma}{A segment of a generated block.}{\mathsf{gen}(s)}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Seg.gen}{text generated from Lean}}
\LeanAlternative{\mathsf{slot}_{i}(t)}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Seg.slot}{a slot, whose text the authors own}}
\end{LeanGrammar}
\end{leangrammar}

\begin{leangrammar}[sum=dwPBXZ-Y8u]{SpliceModel.Piece}{Pieces}
\label{gram:SpliceModel.Piece}\begin{LeanGrammar}
\LeanProduction{p}{A piece of a document.}{\mathsf{text}(s)}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Piece.text}{hand-written text}}
\LeanAlternative{\mathsf{block}_{k}^{v}(r)}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Piece.block}{a generated block: a key, shown in a view}}
\LeanAlternative{\mathsf{checked}_{k}(h)}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Piece.check}{a check that hand-written prose matches the statement with this hash}}
\end{LeanGrammar}
\end{leangrammar}

Regenerating a block fills the rendering's slots with the text the authors
wrote in the old block, slot by slot, and with the rendering's own text for a
slot they have not written:

\begin{leandef}[sum=31yBSoeXn6]{SpliceModel.fill}{Filling slots}
\begin{LeanDisplay}
\LeanClause{\mathsf{fill}(\mathit{old}, []) \;\deq\; []}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.fill}{Fill a rendering's slots: each keeps the authors' text, if they wrote some, else the rendering's}}\\
\LeanClause{\mathsf{fill}(\mathit{old}, \mathsf{gen}(s) \mathbin{::} r) \;\deq\; \mathsf{gen}(s) \mathbin{::} \mathsf{fill}(\mathit{old}, r)}{}\\
\LeanClause{\mathsf{fill}(\mathit{old}, \mathsf{slot}_{i}(t) \mathbin{::} r) \;\deq\; \mathsf{slot}_{i}(\mathit{old}[i] \mathbin{\mathsf{else}} t) \mathbin{::} \mathsf{fill}(\mathit{old}, r)}{}
\end{LeanDisplay}
\end{leandef}

\verb|imprimatur apply| regenerates every block whose key and view the
bundle renders, and leaves every other piece as it
is\leancheck[stmt=5a7f40472101, sig=7546958bfb76]{SpliceModel.apply, SpliceModel.regen}. \verb|imprimatur check|
reports what is not as the bundle says:

\begin{leangrammar}[sum=5g0BdW58g1]{SpliceModel.Diag}{Diagnostics}
\label{gram:SpliceModel.Diag}\begin{LeanGrammar}
\LeanProduction{x}{What \LeanCodeInline{check} reports.}{\mathsf{broken}(k)}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Diag.brokenRef}{the key has no entry}}
\LeanAlternative{\mathsf{untrusted}(k)}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Diag.untrusted}{the key's proof is not trusted}}
\LeanAlternative{\mathsf{missing}_{k}(v)}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Diag.missingView}{the entry has no rendering in the view}}
\LeanAlternative{\mathsf{stale}(k)}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Diag.stale}{the block is not the key's rendering}}
\LeanAlternative{\mathsf{unfaithful}_{k}(v)}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Diag.unfaithful}{the rendering does not read back as the statement}}
\LeanAlternative{\mathsf{nohash}(k)}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Diag.noHash}{the entry has no statement hash}}
\LeanAlternative{\mathsf{drift}(k)}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Diag.drift}{the statement has changed since the prose was checked}}
\end{LeanGrammar}
\end{leangrammar}

A key without an entry is a broken reference; an entry whose proof is not
trusted is reported; a block whose view the entry does not render is
missing it; a block that is not the rendering, with its slots filled, is
stale; a rendering the bundle says does not read back is unfaithful; a check
on a key without a hash has none; and a check whose recorded hash is not the
statement's is drift\leancheck[stmt=ce27f8a301d4, sig=680ab0e4d2e0]{SpliceModel.diags, SpliceModel.check}.

\section{The bundle}\label{sec:bundle}

An entry is what the bundle says of a key: what the key names, its
statement, and its proof. The statement and the proof are not in the file,
and could not be, since the splicer cannot read Lean. The file holds their
images. So an entry is parametrised by how the two are represented:

\begin{leangrammar}[sum=-hBJ5Kt82d]{Imprimatur.Entry}{Entries}
\label{gram:Imprimatur.Entry}\begin{LeanGrammar}
\LeanStructure{\texttt{Entry}(\sigma, \pi)}{An entry: what a bundle says of a key. What the key names, its statement,
as \LeanCodeInline{σ}, and the verdict on its proof, as \LeanCodeInline{π}.}
\LeanField{decl}{\texttt{Decl}}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.Entry.decl}{what the key names}}
\LeanField{statement}{\sigma}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.Entry.statement}{its statement}}
\LeanField{verdict}{\pi}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.Entry.verdict}{the verdict on its proof}}
\end{LeanGrammar}
\end{leangrammar}

Of the statement, the file holds two images. The hash \emph{names} it: two
statements with one hash are one statement, as far as a document can tell.
The views \emph{show} it: each is the statement rendered by the paper's
notation. The \TeX{} is a parameter too, $\tau$: in the file it is \TeX{};
in the model it is \TeX{} with the slots already parsed, a body.

\begin{leangrammar}[sum=B5lL5NWue7]{Imprimatur.Image}{Images of a statement}
\label{gram:Imprimatur.Image}\begin{LeanGrammar}
\LeanStructure{\texttt{Image}(\tau)}{A statement as a document may carry it: the hash that names it, and the
renderings that show it, as \LeanCodeInline{τ}.}
\LeanField{stmtHash}{\mathsf{Option}(\mathit{Hash})}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.Image.stmtHash}{the hash of the statement's meaning: the statement, with every
definition it depends on; none when the dependencies could not be
followed}}
\LeanField{sigHash}{\mathsf{Option}(\mathit{Hash})}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.Image.sigHash}{the hash of the statement as written, the \LeanCodeInline{lean} view; none for a
title}}
\LeanField{views}{\mathsf{Array}(\mathit{View} \times \tau)}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.Image.views}{the renderings, by view}}
\LeanField{errors}{\mathsf{Array}(\mathit{View} \times \mathit{String})}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.Image.errors}{the views that could not be rendered, and why}}
\LeanField{validation}{\mathsf{Array}(\mathit{View} \times \mathsf{Array}(\mathit{String}))}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.Image.validation}{for each view, the formulas in it that do not read back as the
statement}}
\LeanField{macros}{\mathsf{Array}(\mathit{String})}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.Image.macros}{the TeX macros the renderings use}}
\end{LeanGrammar}
\end{leangrammar}

Of the proof, the file holds only a verdict: whether Lean accepted it, and
on what.

\begin{leangrammar}[sum=ccSRTMdW4q]{Imprimatur.Verdict}{Verdicts}
\label{gram:Imprimatur.Verdict}\begin{LeanGrammar}
\LeanStructure{\texttt{Verdict}}{The verdict on a proof: whether Lean accepted it, and on what.}
\LeanField{axioms}{\mathsf{Array}(\mathit{String})}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.Verdict.axioms}{the axioms the proof uses}}
\LeanField{usesSorry}{\mathbb{B}}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.Verdict.usesSorry}{whether it depends on \LeanCodeInline{sorry}}}
\LeanField{cone}{\mathsf{Option}(\texttt{Cone})}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.Verdict.cone}{what the statement depends on}}
\end{LeanGrammar}
\end{leangrammar}

The file is the JSON of $\texttt{Bundle}(\texttt{Image}(\mathit{TeX}),
\texttt{Verdict})$, a map from keys to entries; it is written and read by
the one module that defines these types, and an extractor's bundle survives
the round trip byte for byte. The model's bundle is the same type at the
model's parameters:

\begin{leandef}[sum=w7ghbqB2ku]{SpliceModel.Bundle}{The model's bundle}
\begin{LeanDisplay}
\LeanClause{b \in \mathit{Bundle} \;\deq\; \texttt{Bundle}(\texttt{Image}(\mathit{Body}), \mathbb{B})}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Bundle}{A bundle as the splicer holds it}}
\end{LeanDisplay}
\end{leandef}

\paragraph{Two hashes.} The statement hash is of the statement's
\emph{meaning}: its type, together with every definition it depends on,
with binder names dropped and proofs left out. The signature hash is of the
statement \emph{as written}, the \texttt{lean} view. The two change
independently, and the four cases have four readings:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{l@{\quad}p{3.6cm}@{\quad}p{3.6cm}}
 & \textit{signature same} & \textit{signature changed} \\[2pt]
\textit{meaning same} & nothing changed & renamed or renotated: a warning \\[2pt]
\textit{meaning changed} & a definition it depends on changed & the statement was edited \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The lower-left case is the one a reader cannot see. Replace a stand-in
substitution with the real one, and no theorem about reduction changes a
character, in Lean or in the paper; their meaning does, and their statement
hashes with it. For a theorem the meaning is its proposition under the
definitions it mentions; for a definition, its own equations, so a
definition is in its own cone. A title, a group of rules or a display of
definitions, states what its members state, and is hashed as they are.

\section{What a bundle means}\label{sec:meaning}

A bundle is about an environment, which the splicer never sees. A
\emph{world} is the environment as the extractor presents it: what each key
states and whether its proof is trusted; how a statement is hashed and
rendered; and when a rendering reads back as a statement, up to what the
notation makes invisible (Section~\ref{sec:faithful}).

\begin{leangrammar}[sum=zV8oWA-y7h]{Imprimatur.World}{Worlds}
\label{gram:Imprimatur.World}\begin{LeanGrammar}
\LeanStructure{W \in \texttt{World}(\tau)}{A world: an environment, as the extractor presents it, rendered as \LeanCodeInline{τ}.}
\LeanField{Stmt}{\mathrm{Type}}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.World.Stmt}{statements}}
\LeanField{kind}{\mathit{Key} \to \texttt{Kind}}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.World.kind}{what a key names}}
\LeanField{stmt}{\mathit{Key} \to \mathsf{Option}(\mathit{Stmt}_{W})}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.World.stmt}{what a key states, if it is declared}}
\LeanField{trusted}{\mathit{Key} \to \mathbb{B}}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.World.trusted}{whether a key's proof is trusted}}
\LeanField{hash}{\mathit{Stmt}_{W} \to \mathit{Hash}}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.World.hash}{the hash of a statement's meaning}}
\LeanField{sig}{\mathit{Stmt}_{W} \to \mathit{Hash}}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.World.sig}{the hash of a statement as written}}
\LeanField{render}{\mathit{View} \to \mathit{Stmt}_{W} \to \mathsf{Option}(\tau)}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.World.render}{a statement rendered in a view, when the view applies}}
\LeanField{Reads}{\tau \to \mathit{Stmt}_{W} \to \mathrm{Prop}}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.World.Reads}{when a rendering reads back as a statement}}
\end{LeanGrammar}
\end{leangrammar}

A world has statements and verdicts but no proofs. An image is a function
of the statement alone, so nothing in a bundle can depend on how a theorem
was proved, only on whether it was. An image is \emph{of} a statement when
every claim it makes is true of that statement:

\begin{leanrules}[sum=He57VsxTNN]{Imprimatur.Image.Of}{Images of a statement}
\begin{mathpar}

\inferrule[Image]
  {
    \forall h : \mathit{Hash},\ i.\mathit{stmtHash} = h \implies h = \mathit{hash}_{W}(S) \\
    \forall h : \mathit{Hash},\ i.\mathit{sigHash} = h \implies h = \mathit{sig}_{W}(S) \\
    \forall v : \mathit{View},\ \forall t : \tau,\ (v, t) \in i.\mathit{views} \implies \mathit{render}_{W}(v, S) = t \\
    \forall v : \mathit{View},\ \forall t : \tau,\ (v, t) \in i.\mathit{views} \implies \mathit{problems}_{i}(v) = [] \implies \mathit{reads}_{W}(t, S)
  }
  { \mathit{image}_{W}(S, i) }

\end{mathpar}
\end{leanrules}

An image may say less than the environment knows: a hash it could not
compute, a view it could not render. It may never say something false. Where
it is silent, \verb|check| reports the silence (a missing view, a missing
hash) rather than trusting it. An entry is true of a key when the key names
what it says, its image is of the key's statement, and its verdict, read as
a bit, is the world's; a bundle is true when the entry it gives for each key
is.

\begin{leanrules}[sum=SBbLnxo-_9]{Imprimatur.Entry.True}{True entries}
\begin{mathpar}

\inferrule[Entry]
  {
    e.\mathit{decl}.\mathit{kind} = \mathit{kind}_{W}(k) \\
    \exists S,\ \mathit{stmt}_{W}(k) = S \wedge \mathit{image}_{W}(S, e.\mathit{statement}) \\
    \mathit{trusted}(e.\mathit{verdict}) = \mathit{trusted}_{W}(k)
  }
  { W \models_{k} e }

\end{mathpar}
\end{leanrules}

\begin{leandef}[sum=BO-FFbUkKC]{Imprimatur.Bundle.True}{True bundles}
\begin{LeanDisplay}
\LeanClause{W \models b \;\deq\; \forall k : \mathit{Key},\ \forall e : \texttt{Entry}(\texttt{Image}(\tau), \pi),\ b[k] = e \implies W \models_{k} e}{\leanslot{Imprimatur.Bundle.True}{A bundle is true of a world when the entry it gives for each key is}}
\end{LeanDisplay}
\end{leandef}

Two things follow at once, one for each image. The rendering an entry has
for a view is the key's statement rendered, and reads back as it when the
view has no problems:

\begin{leantheorem}[sum=Xa6Hcbi0zI]{A view shows the statement}{A view shows the statement}
\label{thm:A-view-shows-the-statement}
\LeanProse{Let $\tau, \pi$ be types, $W \in \texttt{World}(\tau)$, $\mathit{trusted} \in \pi \to \mathbb{B}$, $b \in \texttt{Bundle}(\texttt{Image}(\tau), \pi)$, $k \in \mathit{Key}$, $e \in \texttt{Entry}(\texttt{Image}(\tau), \pi)$, $v \in \mathit{View}$ and $t \in \tau$. Assume $W \models b$, $b[k] = e$ and $\mathit{view}_{v}(e.\mathit{statement}) = t$. Then there is $S$ such that (1)~$\mathit{stmt}_{W}(k) = S$, (2)~$\mathit{render}_{W}(v, S) = t$ and (3)~$\mathit{problems}_{e.\mathit{statement}}(v) = [] \implies \mathit{reads}_{W}(t, S)$.}
\end{leantheorem}

And when hashes do not collide, which is the one thing we assume of SHA-256,
one hash means one statement:

\begin{leantheorem}[sum=62mT9X28I5]{The hash names the statement}{The hash names the statement}
\label{thm:The-hash-names-the-statement}
\LeanProse{Let $\tau$ be a type, $W \in \texttt{World}(\tau)$, $S, S' \in \mathit{Stmt}_{W}$, $i, i' \in \texttt{Image}(\tau)$ and $h \in \mathit{Hash}$. Assume $\forall S : \mathit{Stmt}_{W},\ \forall S' : \mathit{Stmt}_{W},\ \mathit{hash}_{W}(S) = \mathit{hash}_{W}(S') \implies S = S'$, $\mathit{image}_{W}(S, i)$, $\mathit{image}_{W}(S', i')$, $i.\mathit{stmtHash} = h$ and $i'.\mathit{stmtHash} = h$. Then $S = S'$.}
\end{leantheorem}

\section{The splicer's laws}

Four laws make \verb|apply| safe to run on a paper the authors are editing.
First, it changes nothing they wrote outside generated blocks. Write
$\lfloor d \rfloor$ for the outline of document $d$: its text, its checks,
and its blocks without their bodies.

\begin{leantheorem}[sum=3p5XLvWAci]{Locality}{Locality}
\label{thm:Locality}
\LeanProse{Let $b \in \mathit{Bundle}$ and $d \in \mathit{Doc}$. Then $\lfloor \mathsf{apply}_{b}(d) \rfloor = \lfloor d \rfloor$.}
\end{leantheorem}

Second, running it again changes nothing more. This needs each rendering's
slots to be distinct, which we write $\mathsf{wf}(b)$\leancheck[stmt=e4ff67e116fd, sig=9d550ab02488]{SpliceModel.WF}.

\begin{leantheorem}[sum=rUqQQUgoM3]{Idempotence}{Idempotence}
\label{thm:Idempotence}
\LeanProse{Let $b \in \mathit{Bundle}$ and $d \in \mathit{Doc}$. Assume $\mathsf{wf}(b)$. Then $\mathsf{apply}_{b}(\mathsf{apply}_{b}(d)) = \mathsf{apply}_{b}(d)$.}
\end{leantheorem}

Third, \verb|check| agrees with \verb|apply|: after it, no block is stale.

\begin{leantheorem}[sum=ajKQ-ezoOC]{Fixed point}{Fixed point}
\label{thm:Fixed-point}
\LeanProse{Let $b \in \mathit{Bundle}$, $d \in \mathit{Doc}$ and $k \in \mathit{Key}$. Assume $\mathsf{wf}(b)$. Then $\mathsf{stale}(k) \notin \mathsf{check}_{b}(\mathsf{apply}_{b}(d))$.}
\end{leantheorem}

Fourth, the text the authors wrote in a slot survives regeneration, as long
as the rendering still has that slot.

\begin{leantheorem}[sum=4WbuqUPxi1]{Slot preservation}{Slot preservation}
\label{thm:Slot-preservation}
\LeanProse{Let $i \in \mathit{SlotId}$, $t \in \mathit{String}$, $\mathit{old} \in \mathsf{List}(\mathit{SlotId} \times \mathit{String})$ and $r \in \mathit{Body}$. Assume $i \in \mathsf{ids}(r)$ and $\mathit{old}[i] = t$. Then $\mathsf{slots}(\mathsf{fill}(\mathit{old}, r))[i] = t$.}
\end{leantheorem}

Accepting the current hashes, \verb|imprimatur accept|, likewise leaves no
drift for \verb|check| to report\leancheck[stmt=1e49679eaefa, sig=692b1df8e983]{SpliceModel.not_drift_after_accept}.

\section{What \texttt{check} guarantees}
\label{sec:check}

What does a paper claim? We say it of the world alone, with no bundle in
sight, and in two stages.

\subsection{Generated mathematics}

A block $\mathsf{block}_{k}^{v}(r)$ claims that the key $k$ states something
Lean proved, that $r$ is that statement rendered in view $v$, with the
authors' text in its slots, and that the rendering reads back as the
statement\leancheck[stmt=f3a293b191c0, sig=a6e738ff2142]{SpliceModel.Piece.True}. Nothing here involves a hash:
the splicer compares the block's text with the rendering in the bundle,
and the bundle's verdict says the proof is trusted. If \verb|check| reports
nothing against a true bundle, warnings included, every block's claim
holds.

\begin{leantheorem}[sum=g6Fxb8r-Rw]{Generated mathematics}{Generated mathematics}
\label{thm:Generated-mathematics}
\LeanProse{Let $W \in \mathit{World}$, $b \in \mathit{Bundle}$, $d \in \mathit{Doc}$, $k \in \mathit{Key}$, $v \in \mathit{View}$ and $r \in \mathit{Body}$. Assume $W \models b$, $\mathsf{check}_{b}(d) = []$ and $\mathsf{block}_{k}^{v}(r) \in d$. Then $W \models \mathsf{block}_{k}^{v}(r)$.}
\end{leantheorem}

Were this all a paper could say, hashes would be unnecessary. They are
there for what the authors write themselves.

\subsection{Attested prose}

A sentence like \emph{preservation holds for well-typed terms} has no
formal content. The only way to tie it to Lean is to record which statement
a person checked it against, and have \verb|check| confirm that statement is
still the one under that key. The key alone will not do: it names a
\emph{declaration}, whatever the development currently says under that
name, and a theorem weakened to $\top$ keeps its name. The rendering will
not do: it changes under a renaming and not under a change of meaning
(Section~\ref{sec:bundle}). What is needed is a name for the meaning that
the paper can carry, and the statement hash is that name. So a check
$\mathsf{checked}_{k}(h)$ claims that $k$ states something Lean proved whose
hash is $h$; and a document is true when each of its pieces is:

\begin{leandef}[sum=dzWzl4ICeZ]{SpliceModel.Piece.True}{What a piece claims}
\begin{LeanDisplay}
\LeanClause{W \models \mathsf{text}(\_) \;\deq\; \top}{\leanslot{SpliceModel.Piece.True}{What a piece claims of a world}}\\
\LeanClause{W \models \mathsf{block}_{k}^{v}(r) \;\deq\; \exists S,\ \exists r',\ \mathit{stmt}_{W}(k) = S \wedge \mathit{trusted}_{W}(k) = \mathsf{true} \wedge \mathit{render}_{W}(v, S) = r' \wedge r = \mathsf{fill}(\mathsf{slots}(r), r') \wedge \mathit{reads}_{W}(r', S)}{}\\
\LeanClause{W \models \mathsf{checked}_{k}(h) \;\deq\; \exists S,\ \mathit{stmt}_{W}(k) = S \wedge \mathit{trusted}_{W}(k) = \mathsf{true} \wedge \mathit{hash}_{W}(S) = h}{}
\end{LeanDisplay}
\end{leandef}

\begin{leantheorem}[sum=YuyPcDgbsu]{Attested prose}{Attested prose}
\label{thm:Attested-prose}
\LeanProse{Let $W \in \mathit{World}$, $b \in \mathit{Bundle}$, $d \in \mathit{Doc}$, $k \in \mathit{Key}$ and $h \in \mathit{Hash}$. Assume $W \models b$, $\mathsf{check}_{b}(d) = []$ and $\mathsf{checked}_{k}(h) \in d$. Then $W \models \mathsf{checked}_{k}(h)$.}
\end{leantheorem}

\begin{leantheorem}[sum=_jhiARqWZl]{The typeset mathematics is true}{The typeset mathematics is true}
\label{thm:The-typeset-mathematics-is-true}
\LeanProse{Let $W \in \mathit{World}$, $b \in \mathit{Bundle}$ and $d \in \mathit{Doc}$. Assume $W \models b$ and $\mathsf{check}_{b}(d) = []$. Then $W \models d$.}
\end{leantheorem}

The hash stands for the statement only when hashes do not collide. Assuming
that, prose checked against a statement still faces that statement: a
reader of the sentence, and of the Lean theorem the person checked it
against, is reading about one thing.

\begin{leantheorem}[sum=5RPDXXmi4l]{Attested prose faces its statement}{Attested prose faces its statement}
\label{thm:Attested-prose-faces-its-statement}
\LeanProse{Let $W \in \mathit{World}$, $b \in \mathit{Bundle}$, $d \in \mathit{Doc}$, $k \in \mathit{Key}$ and $S_{0} \in \mathit{Stmt}_{W}$. Assume $\forall S : \mathit{Stmt}_{W},\ \forall S' : \mathit{Stmt}_{W},\ \mathit{hash}_{W}(S) = \mathit{hash}_{W}(S') \implies S = S'$, $W \models b$, $\mathsf{check}_{b}(d) = []$ and $\mathsf{checked}_{k}(\mathit{hash}_{W}(S_{0})) \in d$. Then $\mathit{stmt}_{W}(k) = S_{0}$.}
\end{leantheorem}

\paragraph{The honest extractor.} The theorem's hypothesis is that the
bundle is true. The bundle an honest extractor makes of a world, hashing
and rendering each statement and recording what its validator finds, is
true of it, provided a rendering the validator finds no problem in reads
back as its statement, which is what Section~\ref{sec:faithful} checks.

\begin{leantheorem}[sum=gvG0W1TTVo]{The bundle of a world is true}{The bundle of a world is true}
\label{thm:The-bundle-of-a-world-is-true}
\LeanProse{Let $W \in \mathit{World}$, $\mathit{views} \in \mathsf{List}(\mathit{View})$, $\mathit{problems} \in \mathit{View} \to \mathit{Stmt}_{W} \to \mathsf{Array}(\mathit{String})$ and $\mathit{keys} \in \mathsf{List}(\mathit{Key})$. Assume $\forall v : \mathit{View},\ \forall S : \mathit{Stmt}_{W},\ \forall r : \mathit{Body},\ \mathit{problems}(v)(S) = [] \implies \mathit{render}_{W}(v, S) = r \implies \mathit{reads}_{W}(r, S)$. Then $W \models \mathsf{bundle}_{W}(\mathit{keys})$.}
\end{leantheorem}

The splicer that runs on real papers works on \TeX{} source, not on
documents already parsed into pieces. Its scanner, its parsing of slots and
its edits are what the model leaves out, and they are property-tested
against the four laws.

\section{Rendering is faithful}
\label{sec:faithful}

The theorems say the text is the \emph{rendering} of the statement, and
that it reads back, $\mathit{reads}_{W}(r, S)$. A reader must indeed
recover the statement from the text: this is Pollack's question of
believing a machine-checked proof~\cite{Pollack98}, and Wiedijk's
\emph{Pollack-consistency}, that printing a term and parsing it back gives
the same term~\cite{Wiedijk12}.

The notation module defines both directions. Read as a grammar, each of its
rules is a mixfix production whose holes are typed by Lean's types and
grouped by precedence. A rendering is \emph{faithful} when it has exactly
one reading in that grammar, and that reading is the statement it was
rendered from, up to what the notation declares invisible: implicit
arguments, coercions, proofs, the names of hypotheses and bound variables,
and the premises and synonyms it names. That is what the world's
$\mathit{reads}$ stands for.

imprimatur checks this for each formula, by translation validation. As the
extractor renders a formula, it also builds the reading the formula should
have: an atom reads as itself, and a rule's rendering as the rule's pattern
with its holes' readings. It then parses the typeset formula, and records
in the bundle, for each view, the formulas with two readings, with none, or
with one that is not the expected reading, a \emph{misread}; \verb|check|
reports them where the formula is. The last is what the check is for. A
renderer that left out the parentheses its holes need would print
$(\lambda r\, q.\, e) = M$ as $\lambda r\, q.\, e = M$, a formula that
reads back uniquely, and wrongly. Made to do so, imprimatur reports six
misreads in a twenty-page paper about another Lean development.

A few forms (a quantifier's binders, a case analysis, an associative chain)
are not given an expected reading, and only what is below them is
compared. There the check rests on the grammar mirroring the renderer, as
both are built from the same rules. In that twenty-page paper, 610 of the
662 formulas with one reading are compared in full, 21 in part, and 31 not
at all.

\paragraph{What the reader knows.} Reading back uses the types of the
formula's variables, which a reader has only if the text gives them. The
\verb|prose| view states the type of each variable it binds (\emph{Let
$b \in \mathit{Bundle}$}). A variable that a grammar or a figure leaves
free is known by its letter, as in BNF, where $p ::= \ldots$ says what $p$
ranges over. With \verb|latex_style categories := strict|, as in this
paper, imprimatur makes the convention exact: a variable whose type has a
metavariable is written with it and its own subscripts and primes, a
metavariable stands for one type, and two variables of one formula never
print alike. Each exception is an error. Appendix~\ref{app:notation} lists
the metavariables and the notation, both generated. A reader who knows
them has the grammar the formulas were checked in.

\section{The trusted base}\label{sec:trusted}

What the theorems and the check do not cover is trusted:
\begin{itemize}
\item Lean's kernel, and the extractor's reading of the environment: that
  the bundle it writes is true of the development. The model says what
  that means, and that an honest extractor's bundle is true; the
  extractor itself is metaprogramming, and is not verified.
\item The hash's resistance to collisions.
\item The splicer's parsing of \TeX{}, of blocks and of slots, which the
  model takes as done; it is property-tested.
\item The parser that reads formulas back, and, where an expected reading
  is incomplete, the grammar's mirroring of the renderer.
\item The notation module, as the paper's dictionary. Lean checks its names,
  not its choice of symbols: a rule that wrote addition as $a - b$ would read
  back faithfully. Appendix~\ref{app:notation} puts the dictionary in the
  paper, where a reader can check it.
\item What the notation declares invisible. A hidden premise makes the
  theorem shown stronger than the one proved; a synonym is an equation the
  notation asserts and nothing checks.
\item The text of a block that is not a formula: the authors' text in
  slots, and the sentences of the \verb|prose| view (\emph{Let \ldots\
  Assume \ldots\ Then \ldots}), whose reading the view fixes.
\item \TeX{}. The checks stop at \TeX{} source, and two macros may print
  alike.
\item For attested claims, the person who checked the prose.
\end{itemize}
