1 A small calculus, generated from Lean
This paper presents a simply-typed λ-calculus whose definitions and results are
in Lean. Everything in a lean... environment below is generated from the
Lean development by imprimatur; the rest is written by hand, and the hand-
written statements are checked against Lean.
1.1 Syntax
Terms are name-carrying: a variable is a name, and an abstraction binds a
name. The grammar is generated from the inductive types. The descriptions
on the right are the authors’: imprimatur keeps them when it regenerates the
grammar, and uses the constructors’ docstrings only for new ones.
τ ::= b the base type
| τ1 → τ2 functions
e ::= x a variable
| λx:τ. e an abstraction, binding x in e
| e1 e2 an application
| () the unit value
1.2 Names, α-equivalence and substitution
We handle names as Pitts does [Pitts(2006)]. The basic operation is swapping:
(a b) · e exchanges the names a and b everywhere in e, bound, binding and
free occurrences alike. Unlike renaming, swapping needs no side condition at a
binder, and it is its own inverse; its definition is structural:
(a b) · x = (a b)(x)
(a b) · (λx:τ. e) = λ(a b)(x):τ. (a b) · e
(a b) · (e1 e2) = ((a b) · e1) ((a b) · e2)
(a b) · () = ()
Two abstractions are α-equivalent when swapping each binder’s name with
a name c fresh for both, written c # . . ., makes their bodies α-equivalent:

Substitution e[u/x] is then defined by recursion on e. At an abstraction
λa:τ. e it first swaps a with a name c fresh for x, a, e and u, so that no free
name of u is captured, and continues in (a c) · e. Swapping preserves the size of
a term, so the recursion terminates, although (a c) · e is not a subterm of the
abstraction.

1.5 Results
A result can be generated, as a sentence; its key can be a title, declared in the
notation module:
Theorem 1 (Swapping is involutive). Let a, b ∈ Name and e ∈ Term. Then
(a b) · ((a b) · e) = e.
Theorem 2 (Values are final). Let v, e ∈ Term. Then ¬(v −→ e).
Or as a rule, from a theorem whose hypotheses become its premises:
e1 −→ e′
1
e1 e2 −→ e′
1 e2
Or the prose can be written by hand and checked: the unit value has only
the base type, that is, if Γ ⊢ () : τ then τ = b. Should the Lean statement of
unit_type change, imprimatur check reports this sentence.
1.6 Type safety
The substitution lemma is proved as Pitts’s paper suggests. Typing is equiv-
ariant: swapping two names throughout a term and its context preserves its
type.
Theorem 3 (Equivariance). Let a, b ∈ Name, Γ ∈ Context, e ∈ Term and
τ ∈ Type. Assume Γ ⊢ e : τ. Then (a b) · Γ ⊢ (a b) · e : τ.
Typing also depends on the context only at the term’s free names, which
gives weakening and shadowing at once. So substitution preserves types: if
Γ, x:τ1 ⊢ e : τ and Γ ⊢ u : τ1, then Γ ⊢ e[u/x] : τ. At an abstraction,
substitution renames the binder to a fresh name by swapping, and equivariance
carries the typing of its body across the renaming; the induction is on the size
of e, which swapping preserves. Type safety follows.
